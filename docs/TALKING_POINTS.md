# Talking points — bringing the map to San Diego

**Goal of the meeting:** not to impress anyone with the model — to get one person with reach to
*use* the map. A win is "I'll share this / bring it to council / look at my district," not "nice
work." Everything below serves that.

## The 30-second version (open with this)
> "San Diego mostly fixes intersections *after* crashes pile up — the City's annual review only
> flags locations with five or more. I built a free, public tool that ranks the intersections
> **below** that line by how likely each is to have a serious crash in the next three years. It's
> validated — its top-500 catches future crash sites about **seven times better than chance**, on
> a year it never trained on — and every site on it is one the City's review doesn't see yet."

## The three points
1. **The gap.** The reactive review finds ~14 sites a year, after harm. This looks *below* the line.
2. **The tool.** Free, open source, filterable by **council district**, re-runs automatically on
   every new crash-data release.
3. **The ask.** (see per-audience below)

## Say the caveats yourself — this is what makes you credible
- *"It's a prioritization tool, not a detector. It mostly surfaces busier corridors with recent
  crashes and higher-risk road design. It misses most future crashes and it's right only some of
  the time."*
- *"It's not new science — the City's systemic safety program keys on similar road features. The
  value is a **ranked, validated, public** list of the sub-threshold sites, by district."*

Leading with these disarms the technical person in the room. Getting *caught* hiding them ends the
meeting. You lose nothing by being the one who says it first.

## Ask them three questions — turns a pitch into a conversation
1. "Does the top of my district's list match corners you already worry about?"
2. "What would make this actually usable to you — a format, a threshold, one specific district?"
3. "Who else should see this?"

## Per audience
- **Circulate San Diego — do this first.** Advocacy nonprofit, works directly with City Council,
  moves faster than any agency, replies to students. They're the amplifier. Ask: *"Would this be
  useful to your Vision Zero work, and would you share it?"*
- **A council office.** Pick the district you can make a personal case in. Ask: *"Can I show your
  staff the flagged corners in [district]?"* Bring that district's list from
  `results/by_council_district.csv`.
- **SafeTREC (UC Berkeley).** They'll go straight to the method. Lead with the honest finding — the
  signal is substantially traffic exposure — and the validation. Technical reviewers trust people
  who report the null.

## The one link + one line to send
> "Free map of the San Diego intersections most likely to have a serious crash next — the ones the
> City's review doesn't flag yet, filterable by council district: **[map URL]**. Independent student
> research; code and method are open."

One deployed link, one honest sentence, one person. That's the whole play.
